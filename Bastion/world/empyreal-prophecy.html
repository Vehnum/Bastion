The Empyreal Prophecy

The last time anyone tried to actually finish the job — and how badly it went

The Empyreal Prophecy was a foretelling that gripped Bastion with hope roughly eight years before Book 1 begins: that a cohort of the city's Imperators would finally destroy the Pit, hell's ultimate source of evil, and reopen the long-sealed way home to Ettera. It failed catastrophically, and the city has not fully recovered from that failure since. This article gathers material scattered across several other pages — it isn't linked from the main index, but is worth reading in full if you've followed the threads around Imogen, Nova, and the political unrest simmering under Bastion's surface.

The assault

Details of the assault itself remain thin — no character has narrated it directly on the page. What's confirmed: a cohort of Imperators, including Imogen and Sol, attempted to push all the way to the Pit itself in pursuit of the prophecy. It failed. The cost was severe enough that Imogen burned the last of her own Igneous Heart's substance to save Sol and several others, becoming one of the Fallen in the process — see The Igneous Heart & Mana for what that transformation actually costs a Great Soul.

Aftermath: the Fallen

Imogen was not the only casualty. Two other Imperators, Zellair and Joranvyn, are now known to have become Fallen in the same incident, alongside her. All three were, at the time, patrons of House Basilisk — the loss of all three of its Imperator patrons in a single incident remains a quiet source of shame for the House. Whatever the full mechanics of the failed assault were, it left at least three of Bastion's most powerful Great Souls hollowed out, unstable, and effectively lost to the city for the better part of a decade; Imogen's own reappearance in Book 1, Chapters 38–40 is the story's only direct look at what became of any of them.

Aftermath: the Deniers

The prophecy's public failure did lasting political damage. It fed ongoing riots and gave rise to the Deniers, a skeptical political movement that argues Bastion's entire founding narrative — the reincarnation cycle, the Great Souls' war against the Pit, all of it — is propaganda used to justify the privileges Great Souls enjoy over ordinary Bastion-born citizens. See Bastion and The Four Houses for how this unrest plays out closer to the story's present day, including its connection to the unrest incident involving Makiros.

Within the Academy, the failure was significant enough to force a curriculum change: a new course, Great Soul Organizations, was added eight years ago and remains part of the standard cohort rotation as of Book 1 — see The Academy for its place alongside the other core subjects.

Why it matters going forward

The Empyreal Prophecy is, functionally, the most recent real attempt at the same goal the Infernarch theory describes: someone finally destroying the Pit and reopening the way to Ettera for good. Its failure is cited directly in Great Souls & Rank as the most recent and public underscoring of just how far out of reach that goal has always been — which makes Scorio's own past-life warning about becoming the Infernarch himself, and the unexplained Herdsmen he's been told not to trust, land very differently in hindsight. Nova, who has first-hand knowledge of what happened to Imogen and was visibly, mutually familiar with her mid-battle, resurfaces in Book 2 as the ruler of the Fiery Shoals — and remains the wiki's best lead on learning more about what actually happened during the assault, should she ever choose to share it.
