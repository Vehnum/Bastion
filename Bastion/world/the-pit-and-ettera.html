The Pit & Ettera

Everything in Bastion is measured by distance from one and hope of reaching the other

Two places anchor the entire cosmology of Bastion, referenced constantly but never explained in one place: the Pit, the source of every drop of mana and every fiend in existence, and Ettera, the home world the city's founders were exiled from. This article gathers what's been established about both — it isn't linked from the main index, but nearly every other page eventually points back to it.

The Pit

The Pit is hell's ultimate source of mana and, per the Great Souls & Rank page, is explicitly named as the source of evil itself. Every grade of mana — Coal through the near-mythical Noumenon — is graded by distance from the Pit: richer grades cannot "travel" as far from their source, which is why Bastion's own ambient supply is almost entirely Coal, with only rare pockets of Copper, while Iron mana has to be artificially concentrated at the Academy. See The Igneous Heart & Mana for the full grading system.

Proximity to the Pit is also dangerous in its own right: the Curse, an ambient mental-impairment effect, intensifies the closer one gets, from mild euphoria and impaired judgment at the edges to hallucinations, unconsciousness, and eventually death deeper in. Only the most powerful Great Souls can approach the Pit itself and still function — which is why Imperators, who burn Noumenon mana found only near the Pit, are so rare (76 in all of Bastion's history) and so significant.

The entire purpose of the Great Soul reincarnation cycle, as far as anyone in Bastion understands it, points toward the Pit: the theoretical endpoint of the whole Path of Ascension is the Infernarch, the Great Soul who finally destroys the Pit outright. See Great Souls & Rank for how this theoretical goal has taken on new urgency by the end of Book 1, and The Empyreal Prophecy for the most recent, most public attempt (and catastrophic failure) to actually do it.

Ettera

Ettera is Bastion's home world — the place its founders came from, and the place the entire reincarnation project is ultimately meant to lead back to. The Dead Portal, one of the two huge ivory structures anchoring Bastion's central sun-wire, is said to be a dormant gateway back to Ettera, closed for longer than anyone can remember. See Bastion for the city's full geography, and The Old Academy, built closest to the Dead Portal, for how that proximity shaped its history.

Imogen's entire Book 1, Chapter 38–40 arc is driven by mistaking a strange mana signal from the old, broken Archspire for the Dead Portal finally reopening — and by her grief on discovering it hasn't. No character has described Ettera itself in any detail; as of Book 1, Chapter 80, it remains purely aspirational, a place nobody currently alive has ever actually seen.

Why this matters

Nearly every major system in the story eventually resolves back to one of these two places: mana grade and the entire Path of Ascension are a function of distance from the Pit; the Academy's whole purpose is training Great Souls to eventually approach it; and the unspoken, thousand-year hope binding Bastion together is that someone will eventually reopen the way to Ettera. Scorio's own past-life warning about the Herdsmen and his path toward becoming the Infernarch (see Great Souls & Rank) puts him, however reluctantly, on the same track the Empyreal Prophecy's cohort was on eight years earlier — a track that has never once succeeded.
