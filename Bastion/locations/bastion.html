Bastion

A dying cylinder, ten years from the end

Bastion is a vast hollow cylinder built into the Rascor Plains, the first and largest region of hell. Its inhabitants live on the inside of the curved wall, held in place by gravity that pulls toward the surface rather than "down" in any fixed direction; a single glowing filament, the sun-wire, runs the length of the central axis and provides all of the city's light.

Geography

The sun-wire is anchored at each end to a huge ivory structure. One end, the Dead Portal, is said to be a dormant gateway back to Bastion's home world, Ettera — closed for longer than anyone can remember. The other end, Hell's Door, opens directly onto hell's sky through four massive gaps, where House-operated skycranes haul cargo across the disorienting 45-degree gravity transition between Bastion and hell proper.

Roughly a third of the cylinder, clustered around the Dead Portal end, has collapsed into the ruins — a wasteland of scorched, cracked buildings and fire-lit chasms, inhabited by scavenger fiends and a handful of human outcasts, including Scorio and Naomi.

The Narrows

A rough, low-income district where House Basilisk operates with relative impunity. Home to the Double, a bar used as a meeting point for Basilisk's operative Dola. Scorio's early allies, street musicians Feiyan and Helena, lived on the edge of the ruins nearby before leaving the district for good after helping him.

Time & light

Bastion has no true day or night; instead, the sun-wire's brightness cycles through named phases used as the city's clock: Clay (darkest) → Rust → Bronze → Amber (brightest) → Second Bronze → Second Rust → Second Clay, followed by an extended dark period. A full year is 384 cycles, marked by a distinctive pulse in the sun-wire's light.

A city on a countdown

Bastion's ruined zone is not static — it is actively expanding, and has been for centuries. What once crept forward by inches a year now visibly advances day to day. At current estimates, the entire city — including the Academy and the Archspire — has perhaps ten years left before it is consumed entirely, ending the reincarnation cycle for good. No one knows the cause for certain: theories range from a severed metaphysical connection to Ettera, to spiritual judgment, to a deliberate countdown built into the world itself.

The oldest buildings — the original palace, basilica, and Academy, all built closest to the Dead Portal — were the first structures lost, meaning the Academy in current use is a replacement, roughly two-thirds of a millennium old.

Politics & unrest

Bastion is nominally governed by the Four Houses, most of whose leadership are reincarnated Great Souls. The city was gripped a few years ago by excitement over the Empyreal Prophecy, which foretold the Imperators would destroy the Pit and reopen the way home to Ettera; its catastrophic failure has fed ongoing riots and a skeptical political movement known as the Deniers, who argue the entire war narrative is propaganda used to justify the privileges Great Souls enjoy over ordinary Bastion-born citizens.

Scars of Imogen's assault

Imogen's Book 1, Chapter 41–42 rampage left entire swathes of the city permanently and nonsensically mutated: staircases running upside down, rooms extruded onto exterior walls, towers skewering buildings without displacing a single stone. The damage is scattered rather than total, but severe enough to change the character of whole Wards; dozens of citizens died in the chaos, and Academy students — including Scorio's group — spent the following Eighthday helping restore order across the city, including quelling a near-riot in Ward 4 fueled partly by the Deniers' rhetoric.

Deepening unrest

By Book 1, Chapters 51–60, the mood has curdled further rather than settled. Streets that used to be busy at all hours now empty out early; Great Souls report open, hostile stares from ordinary citizens who once treated Academy whites as a mark of prestige rather than resentment. A violent incident tied to a man named Makiros in Book 1, Chapter 59 becomes the latest flashpoint for the Deniers' rhetoric, which has shifted from skeptical commentary into something closer to organized anger. Nothing has boiled over into full riot again, but the sense of a city running out of patience — on top of its literal ten-year countdown — is now explicit rather than implied.
