The Chasm

A mile-wide hole that has swallowed every colony ever built inside it

The Chasm is a colossal, roughly circular sinkhole in the Rascor Plains, "diametrically opposite" the Fiery Shoals and about a mile across — large enough that Scorio initially expects a canyon and is startled to find something closer to a bottomless pit. It becomes Manticore's primary Book 2 setting from Chapter 33 onward, after Imogen's Book 1 assault on Bastion stirred up unusual mana activity there.

Geography & the levels

Forested valley floor, thick canopy right up to the rim; twin rivers plunge over the edge as waterfalls that have eroded the cliff walls into hollow caves behind them. Over centuries, Great Souls have sunk vertical shafts through those hollows, and the Chasm's depth is stratified by mana grade in the same order documented on The Igneous Heart & Mana: Coal near the rim (roughly half a mile thick at the Chasm, versus a far deeper Coal layer under Bastion itself), then Copper, Iron, Bronze, Silver, and Gold in descending order. A Blood Baron is known to have descended as far as Sapphire; nobody documented has gone deeper. The Curse (see the Curse) is in full effect at depth — Emberlings can tolerate descending only as far as Iron before feeling its first edges.

The walls below the rim are riddled with ruined wooden platforms, cranes, and buildings built directly into the cliff face by past Great Soul colonies — remnants of a waystation that, per Dameon, bustled with commerce roughly four centuries ago. Every one of these colonies has been wiped out over the centuries, with no explanation and no survivors found; multiple Manticore members independently describe a recurring "sense of being watched" while working the lower levels, taken seriously enough that Flame Vaults order people back to the surface when it grows strong. Neither mystery is resolved as of Book 2, Chapter 40.

Camp Manticore

Manticore's base camp sits at the Chasm's rim, built over the ruins of older, abandoned camps at the same site and standing, as of Book 2, roughly three years. Layout: a central shaft with a crane-wheel elevator for lowering personnel to the lower levels (a slower platform descent, or self-powered descent for those able to manage it), a great hall used for meals, meetings, and indoor training, a stockroom and pantry, and four ten-person dormitories. Chores are communal; there are no servants.

Camp regulars encountered on the page include Jadon, a dry, solemn Flame Vault who gives new recruits their orientation tour; Valt, who operates the elevator's crane wheel; and Crush, a Flame Vault whose Iron-tempered body makes her Manticore's default sparring partner for new recruits.

The hunt for the Font

Manticore's Dread Blaze leader, Dameon, has spent well over half a year directing a search — using memories from a past life spent in one of the Chasm's collapsed colonies — for a specific lost artifact: the Font, said to duplicate, on a personal scale, the process by which Bastion's floating Gold islands draw mana up from the depths. The vault holding it collapsed into what Dameon calls the "Emerald zone" (a grade between Gold and Sapphire on the wiki's own established mana hierarchy) and remained sealed behind rockfall until House Kraken's Pyre Lady Druanna was brought in to help force it open in exchange for exclusive sale rights (Book 2, Chapters 33–38, 40).

Only one Great Soul crew is permitted to work the Chasm at a time — a House Chimera crew is already waiting for Manticore to leave — a tradition rooted in a documented incident roughly a century ago in which several crews shared the Chasm simultaneously and all disappeared, including members who never left the surface.

The vault raid that closes Book 2, Chapter 40 is an unresolved cliffhanger. Dameon and Druanna, along with six other Dread Blazes, descended to the Gold level expecting a two-hour operation; it stretched to nearly three full day-night cycles. When the recovery cage finally surfaced, Dameon was carrying Druanna's body, and Dread Blazes Evelyn, Simeon, Mavel, and Tsing Ma were all missing. What happened, and whether the Font was recovered, is not yet known.
