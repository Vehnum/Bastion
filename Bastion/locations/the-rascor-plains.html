The Rascor Plains

Everything Book 1 couldn't show you, because Bastion is only a hole in the ground

The Rascor Plains are the region of Hell that Bastion is built into — or rather, built down into. Book 1 treats Bastion as effectively the entire known world; Book 2 opens that world up considerably. Bastion sits at what its own inhabitants call "the top of Hell": every one of its four gates leads out in a direction its own geography considers "south," and the Plains beyond stretch for a scale the city's own residents have no real frame of reference for. This article gathers the geography introduced across Book 2, Chapters 9–16.

The Ash Belt

The band of barren land immediately surrounding Bastion. Nothing grows here and no rain falls; the ground is a fine gray dust that is, on inspection, actually inert granulated mana — mostly Coal, with flecks of Copper, Iron, Bronze, and Silver, and rarer traces of Emerald and Sapphire. Nobody in-story has an explanation for why so much mana simply lies unclaimed on the ground for roughly fifty miles around the city, or why it remains inert. Ancient, monumental walls — far older than anyone can date, and possibly older than Bastion itself — ring the city separately from the Ash Belt proper. A day on the Plains is short: roughly half of one full Bastion sun-wire cycle, meaning "daylight" lasts only a few hours at a stretch. True darkness ("midnight") is flagged as the most dangerous period to be caught outside, for reasons not yet explained.

The Rain Wall

A permanent, city-scale storm that rings the Ash Belt at its outer edge, many times taller than Bastion's own walls and roughly a mile deep (sometimes twice that). Its winds are strong enough to lift a person off their feet entirely. It is not weather in the normal sense: it's explicitly stated to be caused by the huge rotating bands of Copper mana that arc overhead from the floating Gold islands (see below) toward Bastion. Crossing it on foot requires a powerful escort — Manticore's Dread Blaze Evelyn gets the fellowship through by physically tethering everyone together with her hair.

The Farmlands

Beyond the Rain Wall lies fertile, cultivated land fed by waterfalls off a world-spanning cliff face, worked by ordinary Bastion-born commoners under the Curse. Unlike the wards inside Bastion, the Farmlands have no towns or villages, only scattered farmsteads, windmills, and fortified Traveler's Barns that double as refuges during fiend incursions. The Curse's effect out here is described bluntly by Manticore's Evelyn as similar to chronic, low-grade intoxication: farmworkers exhibit blank stares, sudden fits of laughter, and lapses of attention, an impairment everyone treats as simply how commoners function this far from Bastion. House Kraken and House Hydra jointly hold most of the Farmlands and post powerful Great Souls — among them Pyre Lady Druanna and Pyre Lord Valtaire — to defend it, since virtually all of Bastion's wealth and food ultimately flows through this land.

The floating Gold islands

Six enormous flying islands, each roughly a mile across and topped with ruined, pre-Bastion castles, orbit the Farmlands on a fixed circuit, each continuously drawing an immense standing column of Gold mana up from the ground below. This process is the literal source of the Rain Wall: the harvested Gold disperses from each island's tower as the great bands of Copper visible overhead. Nobody living understands how the islands' mechanisms work, and deliberately tampering with one's Gold stream is an automatic Red List offense — a single act of tampering, centuries ago, is what caused one island to crash and carve the two-hundred-mile scar that became the Fiery Shoals. An island's passing leaves a trail of supercharged, then rapidly dying, overgrowth in its wake, and stirs up dangerous fiends — the fellowship survives a goldyolk swarm and an Ixithilion attack in exactly this kind of aftermath (Book 2, Chapter 15).

Why this matters

Book 1's entire cast of major players — Praximar, the Autocrators, the Empyreal Prophecy, even Nova herself — is repeatedly shown, once the fellowship actually leaves Bastion, to matter far less to the wider world than the city assumes. A Pyre Lady casually patrolling House Kraken's Farmland holdings rates as more dangerous than Praximar in a straight fight; Bastion's rebellion barely registers as news to travelers a few days out; and Nova's own decision to leave the Fiery Shoals is treated by everyone present as a far bigger event than anything happening back in Bastion. See Book 2 for how this recontextualization plays out chapter by chapter.
